One could argue that human's minds often seek to achieve immediate satisfaction not caring whether their actions are good or bad. **Bullying can be both a conscious and an unconscious behavior that will certainly and in all cases hurt the victim.** Jack bullied and hurt many of the boys on the island. **Due to the complete control of the id with the absence of his ego to judge his behavior, Jack might be captive of his own mind and could not have any other choice.**

Freud divided the human mind into three levels of consciousness: id, ego, super ego. **The ego represents reason and common sense, in contrast to the id which contains the passions.** In Jack's situation, the motives were leading him to achieve pleasure and satisfy his instincts. What was suitable for Jack in every situation was fulfilling his own benefits and advantages, without caring about anyone else. **It was like Jack allowed his id to overpower his ego intentionally and consciously, regardless of the outcome of his behavior.**

There are several conscious and unconscious reasons for performing the act of bullying. **The most apparent reason in Jack's character is seeking power and proving it among all the boys on the island.** When Ralph was elected to be the chief by the vote of majority, Jack was shocked and tried with all the ways to prove that he was the one who deserves to be the leader. Jack targeted the inferior mainly because they cannot defend themselves and on the inhabitant island there was no one to support them. Jack was powerful as he was the leader of the hunters, yet he wanted to control and have power over the whole island. His hunting was not only for food but also for his own pleasures and desires.

**Another reason for Jack being violent is avoiding embarrassment.** Jack is seen as the one trying to adapt the situation they were all put in and searched for survival on the island, while on the other hand Ralph had a bigger vision and was seeking survival from the island. Jack once took all the boys with him to satisfy his desire for hunting and missed a ship that could have rescued them. As the ship left because of Jack's fault of chasing his desires and letting the fire out, the boys started to blame him and his carelessness. When he tried to overcome this embarrassment his aggressive behavior originated once again, {He took a step.. stuck his fist into Piggy's stomach. Ralph made a step forward and Jack smacked Piggy's head.}

**Jack's main purpose of bullying was gaining pleasure and having fun.** He and the boys with him locked Robert and besieged him within a circle of their bodies. They started to act as they were killing a pig, which was Robert. They even sang the chant with Jack's knife waving in the air. **The acting became at the end real and caused the murder of two boys on the island.** They all lost humanity and Jack was remorseless yet he enjoyed all that. In every situation that involved the littleluns, Jack was ready to get rid of them, put them in danger and ignore their suggestions. He was careless and heartless not even to regret and feel sorry about his actions, but it was a pleasure and a source of happiness to his soul.

Lord of the Flies illustrates Piggy as a short and an overweight boy but **the one who has wisdom and science that the boys need to survive and be rescued.** Piggy was well-educated and had a well upbringing that affected his morals and behavior. On the other hand, Jack seemed to be uneducated, ignorant and primitive. This is another reason for acting aggressively through their life on the island. Jack felt that Piggy might be more useful and powerful in this area, he had low self-esteem in his education and intellectuality, so he thought bullying would somehow reduce the difference between them and make himself the more powerful.

Jack behaving as bully caused different consequences on the island. Group violence first appeared at the night on the beach when all the boys thought that Simon was the beast coming towards them. {There were no words, and no movements but the tearing of teeth and claws.} **Even Ralph and Piggy, the boys whom morality surfaced more on their personalities, were involved in the horrible murder of Simon.** The pleasure of getting rid of the beast defeated their conscience and blinded their insight.
Also by the end of the novel when the boys were separated between two leaders and two war teams, all of Jack's boys were seeking their own pleasure of killing and hurting others. **This led to throwing a huge rock towards Ralph and Piggy which caused Piggy's death.**

**The island lost its system and balance.** At first we see an attempt from Jack to maintain civility. {We've got to have rules and obey them. After all were not savages. We are English, and English are the best in everything. So we got to do the right things.} However this sense of civilization and order fades quickly, and Jack always broke the rules and never obeyed them. Jack's greed for desires decreased the chance of getting rescued or even the idea of survival. **The island at the end did not show any sense of civilized citizens but was seen as barbaric and primitive.**

Even if some of Jack's actions were unconsciously performed, he was aware of the consequences of his behavior as the boys were stating that constantly to him so he should have stopped at some point, but nothing kept him away from achieving his own pleasure. **This leads to the big question: After the rescue, will Jack show repentance and regret and how will this incident affect his behavior as a grown up? Will he still be captive of his own mind or will he be aware that he should set himself free?**
